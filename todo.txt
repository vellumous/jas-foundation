JAS Foundation is actively engaged in several communities across the Cape Flats; namely, Athlone, Elsies River, Guguletu, Kenilworth and Manenberg.

## The Beauty of Bowen

[Vellum, add this content to the current The Value of Bowen page, below the "Why we teach it here" section, decide the layout/style based on the current look and feel/vibe]

The beauty of Bowen is its simplicity, you only need your hands, knowledge of the modality, and understanding of anatomy and physiology. Bowen is a portable skill, making it easily accessible and beneficial to communities that lack resources. In addition, it can provide the possibility of a dignified livelihood and community service.


The Next Five Years (2027 to 2032)

Over the next five years, JAS Foundation will simultaneously consolidate and expand its operations and programmes, across the Western Cape Province. The Foundation is working to develop a scalable model, which we can roll out in communities across South Africa. The model should be: community driven, increase access to social and community services, and contribute to job-creation.

To this end, key components of the JAS Foundation’s work in the coming years are:

Training Courses. Each year, the ‘Hands of Change’ training programme aim to deliver several courses concurrently; namely, Bowen Practitioners (foundation course); Bowen Advance, Bowen Masters, and Bowen instructors. Trainees are supported through mentorship and personal development coaching. Other healing modalities to be offered, include: scar work, trauma release, etc. The Foundation also intend to offer training courses in rural Western Cape by 2032.

Clinics. We plan to establish JAS Foundation Clinics in communities across the Western Cape; operated and managed by JAS Foundation trainees and practitioners. We will establish Teaching Clinics, Community Clinics, and Private Clinics.

  Teaching Clinic. A Teaching Clinic is similar to the concept of a teaching hospital. At JAS Foundation Teaching Clinics, our trainees and practitioners have an opportunity to practice under supervision, while they are learning. The Teaching Clinic is open to all, thereby making Bowen therapy accessible to local community members. There is currently one Teaching Clinic in Athlone, and a second Teaching Clinic is scheduled to open in Khayelitsha during 2027.

  Community Clinics. Community Clinics are set up by JAS Foundation trainees, to do community service in their respective community, where they offer Bowen therapy to people in need.

  Private Clinics. There are two types of private clinics: one, private practices set up by the JAS Foundation practitioners once they are qualified; and two, Private Clinics established by the JAS Foundation to serve clients who can pay for treatment. This will help the Foundation to raise funds to cross subsidise the Hands of Change training programme; as well as provide a space for qualified practitioners who do not have the space or resources to open a private practice, to generate an income. The first Private Clinic is planned to open in Wynberg during 2027.

Research. In the last 100 years, we have seen major innovation and technological advance, which provides an opportunity to improve our health and healing treatment and services. There are currently insufficient scientific research and data on Bowen Technique and its impact. In the next ten years, the JAS Foundation wants to develop a research programme using innovation and technology to document, learn, and contribute to the body of scientific knowledge on restorative medicine and healing modalities.

‘A practitioner in every town and township’

JAS Foundation seeks to promote healing modalities focussed on restorative medicine; and to build the capacity of local institutions and practitioners; thereby enabling well-being and health in poor and disadvantage communities across the Western Cape and South Africa.

Over the next decade we aim to build a network of 300 Bowen practitioners, serving over 150 towns across the Western Cape Province.

The JAS Foundation started informally when Karrima Nortje began doing Bowen therapy on family and friends.

Over a few years (2018 to 2024), this grew into a pop-up clinic in Kewtown Athlone; located in Shahieda Bower’s living room; with neighbours from surrounding communities coming for a Bowen treatment. The news spread by word-of-mouth, and soon the sheer volume of clients coming through Shahieda’s living room was simply overwhelming. The situation required a more durable solution, that is, to train local practitioners. (see the ‘Building Bridges’ article link)

In August 2024, the JAS Foundation was launched by the three founders –

Karrima Nortje, jasmin nordien, and Tahirih Michot; in honour of their mothers:

Jean Nortje, Amina Nordien, and Shelly (Rochelle) Cook. (see posters of moms, link) The mission: To make healing modalities accessible to marginalised communities; by building a network of health-care practitioners over the next decade. [link to YouTube clip – the launch]



In 2024, JAS Foundation entered into a partnership with Braitex factory in Atlantis, and operated on a co-pay model, where both the factory and its employees financially contribute to treatment costs. The partnership with a company was experimental, and sought to make Bowen treatment accessible and affordable to workers.

In 2025, the JAS Foundation piloted the ‘Hands of Change’ training programme; starting with eight trainees attending a Bowen Practitioner’s course (January 2025). Six trainees successfully completed the course in November 2025.

In addition, we established a pro bono Teaching Clinic in Athlone, which offers Bowen therapy to the surrounding community, one Saturday a month. And, trainees set up several Community Clinics in their neighbourhood.

In 2026, There was changes in the leadership team; Tahirih Michot left, and Constance (Connie) Formson and Sharron Marco-Thyse joined the board. Furthermore, we expanded the training programme to include the Bowen Advance course, starting with three practitioners.
